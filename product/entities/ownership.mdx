The **Ownership** tab lets you track who owns each entity and who controls it. Commenda records both **shareholders** (individuals or corporate entities holding equity) and **ultimate beneficial owners (UBOs)** — the natural persons who ultimately own or control the entity, as required by KYC and beneficial ownership reporting rules in most jurisdictions.

<Note>
The redesigned Ownership experience is rolling out progressively. If you do not yet see the features described below, your workspace is still on the legacy cap table view — your account team will let you know when the new experience is enabled.
</Note>

## Viewing ownership

1. Go to **Entities** and select an entity.
2. Click the **Ownership** tab.

The Ownership tab is organized into three sections:

- **Shareholders** — every individual and corporate entity that holds equity in the entity, with their ownership percentage, relationship type, and stock class.
- **Ultimate beneficial owners** — the natural persons who own or control the entity, either directly or indirectly through corporate shareholders. UBOs are computed automatically based on the shareholding tree and your jurisdiction's UBO threshold.
- **Recent documents** — recently uploaded entity documents (incorporation certificates, share certificates, register extracts, etc.).

The header shows the total ownership recorded against the entity. Commenda enforces a **100% ownership budget** — you cannot add or edit a shareholder if the resulting total would exceed 100%.

## Adding a shareholder

Click **Add shareholder** and choose whether the new shareholder is an individual or another entity in your workspace.

### Individual shareholder

For an individual, provide:

- **First and last name**
- **Date of birth**
- **Nationality**
- **Ownership percentage** (0–100)
- **Relationship type** — Founder, Investor, Employee, Board member, Advisor, or Other
- **Stock class** (optional) — pick an existing class on the entity, or stage a new one inline (see [Stock classes](#stock-classes))
- **Significant control** (optional) — mark the person as exercising significant control. Toggle this on to record them as a beneficial owner; pick a [control type](#beneficial-owner-control-types) to describe the basis for control.

If the person already exists as a key person on this entity (or anywhere in your workspace), the picker will offer to reuse that record so you do not create a duplicate. The new shareholding is linked to the existing person rather than creating a separate one.

<Note>
**Significant control gates beneficial owner status.** A shareholder is recorded as a beneficial owner only when **Significant control** is toggled on. Crossing the country's UBO threshold by ownership percentage alone no longer auto-promotes the person — you make the call explicitly when adding or editing the shareholder. The threshold is still surfaced as guidance to help you decide.
</Note>

### Corporate shareholder

For a corporate shareholder, pick another entity from the same workspace and provide:

- **Ownership percentage** (set to **0** to record a control-only relationship — see [Control-only corporate shareholders](#control-only-corporate-shareholders))
- **Relationship type** — Parent, Investor, or Other
- **Stock class** (optional) — pick an existing class on the entity, or stage a new one inline
- **Significant control** (optional) — toggle on if the corporate shareholder exercises significant control over this entity

After the corporate shareholder is added, use [Inherit beneficial owners from parent](#inheriting-beneficial-owners-from-a-parent) to propagate any of the parent's UBOs down as indirect UBOs of this entity.

<Info>
Ownership cycles are rejected at write time. You cannot, for example, make Entity A a shareholder of Entity B if Entity B is already a direct or indirect shareholder of Entity A.
</Info>

## Inheriting beneficial owners from a parent

When a corporate entity becomes a shareholder, its UBOs do **not** propagate automatically. Inheritance is an explicit action you take per parent so the indirect-ownership graph reflects deliberate choices rather than automatic recomputes.

To inherit:

1. Open the **Ultimate beneficial owners** section on the entity that has the corporate shareholder.
2. Click **Inherit from parent** and pick the corporate shareholder.
3. Tick the parent UBOs to propagate. For each one, choose the [control type](#beneficial-owner-control-types) that describes how they control this entity (typically **Indirect ownership**, but you can pick **Other significant control** for non-equity propagation).
4. Save.

Each inherited UBO appears in the **Ultimate beneficial owners** section with a path back to the parent corporate shareholder. A single person can appear via multiple paths (e.g. owns this entity directly **and** indirectly through a holding company); each path carries its own control type, which you can edit independently.

<Note>
Inheriting beneficial owners does not retroactively re-run when the parent's UBOs change. If a parent gains a new UBO and you want it to propagate to the child, repeat the **Inherit from parent** action — only the new selections are added, existing inherited records are left untouched.
</Note>

## Control-only UBOs

Some individuals control an entity without holding any equity — for example, a person who can appoint or remove the majority of the board. To record this:

1. Click **Add shareholder** → **Individual**.
2. Enter their details and set **Ownership percentage** to **0**.
3. Toggle **Has significant control** on and pick a **Control type**.

The person appears in the **Ultimate beneficial owners** section but not in the **Shareholders** list, since they hold no equity.

### Control-only corporate shareholders

A corporate entity can also exercise significant control without holding equity — for example, a holding company that has board appointment rights over a sister entity. To record this, add the corporate shareholder with **Ownership percentage** set to **0** and **Significant control** toggled on. The link appears as a dashed edge on the [Corporate structure](/product/entities/corporate-structure) org chart to distinguish it from equity ownership.

## Beneficial owner control types

Each UBO carries a control type for each path through which they qualify. Commenda supports the control types most commonly required by global beneficial ownership registers:

- **Direct ownership** — the person directly owns shares in the entity
- **Indirect ownership** — the person owns the entity through one or more intermediate corporate shareholders
- **Other significant control** — non-equity control (e.g. board appointment rights, voting trusts)

A UBO can have:

- A **direct anchor** control type — assigned when they are recorded as a direct beneficial owner of this entity (set on the shareholder form or via **Add beneficial owner** in the UBO list).
- One or more **inherited paths**, one per parent corporate shareholder through which they propagate down. Each path has its own control type.

The UBO row shows the strongest control basis across all paths. Click into the detail panel to review and edit each path independently, including overriding the control type per parent.

The detail panel also shows their address, nationality, date of birth, masked SSN (last four digits only), and the roles they hold across the entity (director, officer, shareholder, UBO, authorized signatory).

## UBO thresholds

The UBO threshold is the percentage at which most jurisdictions require a person to be reported as a beneficial owner. Commenda surfaces the threshold for the entity's **country of incorporation** as guidance on the shareholder form — for example:

- 25% for most EU countries, the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, and Australia
- 10% for India, Chile, Argentina, and many Caribbean jurisdictions
- 5% for Colombia, Ghana, Nigeria, and South Africa
- Any positive ownership for Ecuador and Guatemala

Whether someone is recorded as a UBO is driven by the **Significant control** flag, not by the threshold alone. The threshold is informational — you can mark someone with a 5% holding as a UBO if local rules or your internal policy require it, or leave a 30% shareholder unmarked if they have no significant control. Thresholds are surfaced automatically per entity; you do not need to configure them.

## Stock classes

If an entity has multiple classes of stock (Common, Preferred Series A, etc.), you can define each class on the entity and assign it to individual shareholdings.

Each stock class has:

- **Class name** (e.g. "Common", "Preferred Series A")
- **Type** (Common or Preferred)
- **Initial shares authorized** — the total number of shares authorized for issue under this class

Stock classes are created and listed per entity. Once a class exists on an entity, it appears in the **Stock class** dropdown when adding or editing a shareholder on that entity. Classes that are still attached to a shareholding cannot be deleted — reassign or remove the shareholdings first.

### Creating a stock class while adding a shareholder

You can stage a new stock class directly from the shareholder form instead of pre-creating it:

1. On the **Add shareholder** or **Edit shareholder** form, open the **Stock class** dropdown.
2. Click **+ Add stock class** and fill in the name, type, and initial shares authorized.
3. Save the shareholder.

The new class is created together with the shareholding in a single atomic write. If you cancel the form, no stock class is created — there are no orphan classes left behind.

### Applying a stock class to existing unassigned shareholders

When you assign a stock class to a shareholder, you can also reassign every other shareholder on the entity that does not yet have a class to the same class. This is useful the first time you add classes to an entity that already has shareholders on the legacy "no class" default.

Toggle **Apply to other unassigned shareholders** on the form before saving. Every shareholding currently on no class is reassigned to the class you picked or staged, in the same write as the shareholder you are editing. Shareholdings that already have a class are left untouched.

## Non-controlled entities and ownership

If a corporate shareholder is a [non-controlled entity](/product/entities/creating-entities#adding-an-existing-entity), it still appears as a shareholder of the entity below and is included in the [Corporate structure](/product/entities/corporate-structure) org chart. However, Commenda does not walk through a non-controlled parent when computing inherited UBOs — only ownership through entities you control contributes to the indirect-ownership UBO calculation. This keeps reporting focused on the people and entities you actually manage.

## Editing and removing shareholders

Use the row actions on each shareholder to update or remove them.

- **Editing an individual** to 0% ownership is supported as long as you also toggle **Significant control** on — that turns the row into a [control-only UBO](#control-only-ubos). If the person should no longer be on the entity at all, **Remove** them instead.
- **Editing a corporate shareholder** to 0% is allowed only when **Significant control** is on (a [control-only corporate shareholder](#control-only-corporate-shareholders)). Otherwise, remove the corporate shareholder if it no longer holds equity.
- **Removing a shareholder** also removes any beneficial-owner records that only existed because of that shareholding. UBOs that have other paths into the entity are preserved.
- **Removing a UBO directly** (without removing their underlying shareholding) clears their direct anchor on the entity. If the same person also has inherited paths from a parent corporate shareholder, those paths remain — they are removed only when the parent shareholding is removed or you re-run **Inherit from parent** with the person unticked.

### Previewing the impact of removing a person

Before deleting a person from your workspace, open the person's detail panel and click **Preview delete**. The preview lists every entity where the person is a key person or UBO, and flags any inherited UBO records that would cascade away because all of their feeding paths run through entities you are removing. Use this to confirm the change before committing.

## Exporting an ownership summary

Click **Export** on the Ownership tab to download a PDF summary of the entity's ownership and control. The document includes:

- A cover page with entity name, incorporation country, and report date
- The full shareholder register with ownership percentages and stock classes
- All ultimate beneficial owners with their control type and source
- Snapshot metadata so the report is suitable for sharing with banks, auditors, and regulators

The PDF contains every shareholder and UBO regardless of pagination on screen.

## Org structure view

For companies with multiple related entities, open the **Corporate structure** view to see the full ownership graph as a diagram. See [Corporate structure](/product/entities/corporate-structure) for details.
